# Drixel SA legal readiness — 7 October 2026

The four React policy pages have been expanded using South African consumer, electronic-commerce and privacy law. This is a researched implementation, not a legal opinion or certification of compliance. A policy cannot substitute for actual business processes. Confirm the seller facts and have a South African legal practitioner review the final factual version.

## Owner actions before treating legal setup as complete

1. **Admin → Settings → Store → Seller & privacy details:** publish the actual seller full name and legal status; business physical address; address accepting legal service; telephone/email. For a legal person, add registration number, place of registration and office bearers. Add VAT details only if registered. Do not invent company registration or claim memberships that do not exist. A sole proprietor must identify the actual proprietor rather than supplying only the trading name.
2. **Information Officer:** identify the responsible party and officer, register the officer as required through the Information Regulator, and publish the privacy contact. The store setting does not perform this registration.
3. **PAIA:** prepare the applicable section 51 private-body manual with actual records, contacts and prescribed request procedures using the Regulator’s current template. Publish it and add its HTTPS URL. This policy is not a PAIA manual. Confirm language/publication duties applicable to your business.
4. **Consumer complaints:** verify Consumer Goods and Services Industry Code participation/registration requirements with CGSO, register where applicable, and publish actual memberships/code details. A complaint link does not claim current accreditation.
5. **Delivery:** confirm courier services, production and delivery timeframes against real capacity. Publish any longer preorder period before purchase and obtain agreement. Verify any PAXI collection point workflow. Country/currency selection alone must not imply a delivery service exists.
6. **Returns/refunds:** nominate a working return address and staff procedure. Honour applicable seven-day ECTA cooling-off and full refund within 30 days; distinguish six-month CPA defective-goods remedies at supplier expense. Do not apply unworn/original-packaging or sale exclusions to statutory quality rights. Record requests promptly even while collection instructions are pending.
7. **Prices/taxes:** confirm VAT status, full checkout totals and international duty disclosures. Never describe the seller as VAT registered unless it is. Do not collect undisclosed costs after acceptance.
8. **Privacy operations:** maintain a data map, retention schedule, access/deletion process and processor agreements. Verify overseas transfer grounds under POPIA section 72 for Google, Cloudflare, GitHub, Resend, Cloudinary and other providers actually used. Naming a provider does not verify its agreement or safeguards.
9. **Marketing:** send promotional messages only to a lawful audience, preserve opt-in evidence, honour unsubscribe/suppression and identify the sender. Do not disguise advertising as an order/service update. Verify live delivery separately; policy updates do not fix the email queue.
10. **Security incidents:** maintain an incident-response plan and notify the Regulator and affected people as required by POPIA section 22, as soon as reasonably possible. An admin passkey does not fulfil all organisational privacy duties.
11. **Records:** preserve order confirmations, receipts, refunds and policy versions relevant to purchases. If optional analytics/ad trackers are later added, review the notice and lawful processing basis before enabling them.

## Research basis

- Consumer Protection Act 68 of 2008: sections 16, 19–20, 22–23, 48–51, 55–56, 61 and complaint remedies, where applicable: https://www.gov.za/documents/consumer-protection-act
- Electronic Communications and Transactions Act 25 of 2002, consumer chapter: sections 42–44 and 46–48. Section 45 was repealed by POPIA; do not treat it as current marketing authority: https://www.gov.za/documents/electronic-communications-and-transactions-act
- POPIA 4 of 2013, processing conditions, transparency, security, rights, direct marketing and overseas transfers: https://www.justice.gov.za/legislation/acts/2013-004.pdf
- Information Regulator guidance and forms: https://inforegulator.org.za/popia/ and https://inforegulator.org.za/paia/
- Consumer Goods and Services Ombud, industry code and complaint assistance: https://www.cgso.org.za/

The application shows incomplete seller disclosures rather than fabricating them. It retains statutory rights and avoids blanket liability waivers, compulsory vouchers, automatic no-refund sale exclusions and claims that courier handover ends responsibility. Policies are linked before order submission and can be printed/saved.
